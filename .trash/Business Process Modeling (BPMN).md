# 1. Definition of Process Discovery

**Process Discovery** is the act of gathering information about an existing business process and organizing it into a formal **As-is process model**.

- **Goal:** The primary objective is to create a model that is both **correct** (reflecting reality) and **complete** (covering all paths and exceptions).
- **Role in BPM:** It acts as the bridge between identifying a process and analyzing it for improvements.
# 2. Information Gathering Techniques

The lectures identify three main categories of discovery techniques used to collect the necessary data from the organization:

1. **Evidence-Based Discovery:** Gathering information from existing documentation, such as standard operating procedures (SOPs), or by extracting data directly from IT systems that support the process.
2. **Interview-Based Discovery:** Speaking directly with process participants and actors to understand their specific tasks and the "pain points" they encounter.
3. **Workshop-Based Discovery:** Bringing together different stakeholders in a group setting to reach a consensus on how the process currently functions from start to finish.
# 3. Modeling as a Tool for Discovery

While BPMN is the "language" used, your notes emphasize that **Business Process Modeling** within this phase is an analytical representation designed to better understand the organization.

- **Visual Representation:** It maps out the connecting activities, events, and resources associated with a product or service.
- **The "As-is" Output:** The result of this discovery is the **As-is process model**, which visually captures breakdowns and areas of risk before any changes are made.
# 4. Advantages of Successful Process Discovery

Properly discovering and modeling a process provides several strategic benefits:

- **Common Understanding:** Ensures everyone in the organization agrees on how work is currently done.
- **Strategic Alignment:** Helps align daily operations with the overall business strategy.
- **Communication:** Improves how the process is explained to new employees or IT developers.
- **Control and Consistency:** Increases the ability to standardize work across different departments.
- **Efficiency and Competitive Advantage:** Identifies where the organization is losing time or money, allowing it to stay ahead of competitors.
