The **BPM Life Cycle** is a continuous, circular process used to manage and improve business operations. It ensures that work is performed consistently and that organizations take advantage of improvement opportunities.

# 1. The Six Phases of the BPM Life Cycle

![[The 6-Phase BPM Life Cycle.png]]

1. **Process Identification:** Organizations align BPM objectives with their overall strategies and goals. All existing processes are identified and prioritized to determine which key processes require immediate attention. This results in a **process architecture**.
2. **Process Discovery:** Analysts gather information about existing processes using techniques like document analysis, interviews, or workshops. This information is organized into an **"As-is" process model**, which serves as an accurate visual of the current state.
3. **Process Analysis:** The "As-is" model is scrutinized to find strengths and weaknesses.
    - **Qualitative Analysis:** Identifies waste and redundancies to be eliminated.
    - **Quantitative Analysis:** Focuses on measurable data like time, cost, and performance statistics.
4. **Process Redesign:** Based on the analysis, the process is reworked to improve efficiency. This results in a **"To-be" process model**, which predicts the impact of changes before they are made. This can be done incrementally (Continuous Process Improvement) or through a total rework (Business Process Re-engineering).
5. **Process Implementation:** The redesigned process is executed. **Systemic implementation** uses specific software and technologies, while **non-systemic implementation** does not rely on dedicated BPM tools.
6. **Process Monitoring and Controlling:** Once implemented, the process is continuously tracked using Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) such as duration, cost, and error rates. Insights gained here provide the data needed to start the cycle again for further improvement.

# 2. As-is vs. To-be Process Models

- **As-is Model:** Captures the current state of the process and helps visualize breakdowns and risks.
- **To-be Model:** Represents the future state and allows for simulation of improvements before implementation.

# 3. Business Process Management Suites (BPMS)

A BPMS is the technical infrastructure that supports this entire life cycle. It provides a model-driven approach that allows business and IT professionals to collaborate on process discovery, design, implementation, and monitoring. Key features often include process modeling, reporting, and workflow management.
