**Process Implementation and Monitoring** represents the final stages of the BPM life cycle, where redesigned processes are put into practice and their performance is continuously tracked to ensure they meet organizational goals.

# 1. Process Implementation (Phase 05)

This phase focuses on transforming the "To-be" process model into an active operational reality. There are two primary ways to execute implementation:

- **Systemic Implementation:** Relies on specific software and technologies to execute and manage the process. This often involves **Business Process Management Suites (BPMS)**, which provide the technical infrastructure for process design, execution, and automation.
- **Non-Systemic Implementation:** Does not use dedicated BPM tools; instead, the process is carried out through manual coordination or existing general-purpose software.
- **Decision Criteria:** Organizations choose between these methods based on the specific nature of the business process and the resources they have available.

# 2. Process Monitoring and Controlling (Phase 06)

After implementation, processes must be tracked and measured on a continuous basis to identify if they are functioning as intended.

- **Common Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):**
    - **Time:** Tracking the duration of the process to identify delays.
    - **Cost:** Measuring the expenses incurred during execution.
    - **Capacity:** Assessing the volume of work the process can handle.
    - **Quality:** Tracking errors or exceptions that adversely affect customer satisfaction.
- **The Feedback Loop:** The information collected during monitoring serves as the trigger for the next BPM life cycle. It helps managers determine if the process needs incremental improvements (Continuous Process Improvement) or a total rework (Business Process Re-engineering).

# 3. The Support of BPM Suites (BPMS)

A BPMS acts as the leading application infrastructure to support these phases.

- **Model-Driven Approach:** It allows business and IT professionals to work together more collaboratively during implementation than other methods.
- **Reporting and Analysis:** BPMS features include **Process Reporting**, which generates reports and graphs based on KPIs to identify inefficient processes, bottlenecks, and unproductive employees.
- **Workflow Management:** It focuses on streamlining the coordination of tasks related to the work a business does.
