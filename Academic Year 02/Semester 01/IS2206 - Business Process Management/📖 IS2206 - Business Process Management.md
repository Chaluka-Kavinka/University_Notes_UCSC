#Lesson-Plan 


> [!NOTE] Goal
> The primary aim of this unit is to provide you with comprehensive knowledge of the **organizational practices of Business Process Management (BPM)**. Through various examples, the course intends to teach you how to recognize business processes within an organization and apply that knowledge to **re-engineer** those processes for better efficiency.

---
# Intended Learning Outcomes (Learning Objectives)

Upon completing this module, you are expected to be able to:

- **Describe** the different phases of the BPM life cycle.
- **Model** "As-is" (current state) and "To-be" (future state) processes using **BPMN** (Business Process Model and Notation).
- **Assess** how IT can support and enhance business process management.
- **Identify** weaknesses in existing processes and apply improvement techniques or best practices to resolve them.
- **Apply** change management theories to various organizational contexts.
- **Improve** your professional communication skills within group settings.

# Topics Covered

The shared lecture notes cover the foundational stages of the BPM framework and the technical details of process modeling:

1. [[Introduction to Business Processes]]
	Understanding what a business process is (a collection of events, activities, and decisions) and its core elements, such as actors, physical/immaterial objects, and the difference between **outcomes** and **value**.

2. [[The BPM Life Cycle]]
	A deep dive into the six phases:
    - **Process Identification**.
    - **Process Discovery** (gathering info for "As-is" models).
    - **Process Analysis** (Qualitative and Quantitative).
    - **Process Redesign** (Continuous improvement vs. Re-engineering).
    - **Process Implementation** (Systemic vs. Non-systemic).
    - **Process Monitoring and Controlling**.

3. [[Process Identification & Architecture]]
	Focusing on key processes, the designation and evaluation phases, and designing a **Process Architecture** using sequential or hierarchical relationships. This includes the **Dijkman Approach** for identifying case types and functions.

4. [[BPM Suites (BPMS)]]
	The role of application infrastructures in supporting the BPM life cycle and their key features like workflow management and process reporting.

5. [[Business Process Modeling (BPMN)]]
	- **Modeling Levels:** Descriptive (Level 1), Analytical (Level 2), and Executable (Level 3).
    - **BPMN Elements:** Detailed usage of **Swim lanes** (Pools and Lanes), **Flow Objects** (Events, Activities/Tasks, Gateways), **Connecting Objects** (Sequence vs. Message flows), and **Artifacts** (Data objects, Groups, Annotations).
