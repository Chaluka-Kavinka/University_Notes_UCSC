**Process Redesign** involves rethinking and reorganizing business processes to improve the quality of products and services. The goals are to adapt to change, improve efficiency, address performance issues, and enable innovation.

# 1. The Devil’s Quadrangle

Analysts must balance four competing performance dimensions when redesigning. This framework highlights that improving one dimension often negatively impacts another:

- **Time:** Minimizing process duration.
- **Cost:** Reducing operational expenses.
- **Quality:** Maximizing accuracy and effectiveness.
- **Flexibility:** Increasing adaptability to changing requirements.

# 2. Heuristic Process Redesign

This methodology refines existing processes using a fixed list of redesign rules across seven elements:

- **Customer Heuristics:** Moving control to the customer, reducing contacts, or integrating with supplier processes.
- **Process Operation Heuristics:** Eliminating unnecessary activities, removing batch processing, or combining small tasks.
- **Process Behavior Heuristics:** Resequencing tasks, executing activities in parallel, or isolating exceptional cases from normal flow.
- **Organization Heuristics:** Centralizing geographically dispersed resources, empowering workers with decision-making authority, or adding extra resources if capacity is low.
- **Information & Technology Heuristics:** Adding controls to check correctness, buffering external data, and automating manual activities.
- **External Environment Heuristics:** Using trusted third parties for information or outsourcing parts of the process.

# 3. Product-Based Design

Unlike heuristic redesign, this aims at **radically rethinking** the process. It ignores the existing process entirely and reasons backward from the characteristics of the product or service to be delivered. This allows for the creation of the leanest possible process by omitting old constraints.

# 4. Case Study: Ford Motors

Ford’s manual Accounts Payable process was inefficient, requiring 500 clerks. In the "As-is" state, three separate documents (purchase order, receiving document, and invoice) had to match before payment.

- **The Redesign:** Ford used Business Process Re-engineering (BPR) to create a digital database.
- **The Result:** When goods arrived, Material Control matched them against the digital order immediately; if they matched, the computer authorized payment automatically. This eliminated the need for invoices and significantly reduced staff.
