A business process is a collection of related **events, activities, and decisions** involving actors and resources that collectively lead to an outcome of value for an organization or its customers. 

These coordinated activities work together within an organizational and technical environment to realize a specific business goal.

# 1. The Core Elements of a Process

To understand the "anatomy" of a process, we look at these building blocks:

- **Events:** These happen automatically and have no duration. They trigger the execution of activities.
    - _Example:_ The **arrival of equipment** at a construction site.
- **Activities & Tasks:** Work initiated by an event. A "Task" is an atomic activity—one single unit of work that cannot be broken down further.
    - _Example:_ An engineer **inspecting the equipment**.
- **Decision Points:** Moments in time when a choice is made that affects how the process is executed.
    - _Example:_ Deciding **whether to return the equipment** or keep it after inspection.
- **Actors:** The people, organizations, or software systems involved in performing the work.
- **Objects:** These can be physical (like materials) or immaterial (like electronic records).
- **Outcomes:** The execution of a process leads to one or several outcomes, which can be **positive** or **negative**.
- **Sub-Processes:** Self-contained, composite activities that can be broken down into smaller units of work.

# 2. Outcome vs. Value

While often used interchangeably, they have a symbiotic relationship in a business context:

- **Outcome:** The measurable results of your product or service (e.g., "Order Fulfilled").
- **Value:** What the customer specifically perceives as beneficial based on their goals and pain points. An outcome only provides value if it solves the customer's specific problem.

# 3. Categories of Business Processes

Organizations generally manage three types of processes to meet operational and financial goals:

1. **Core Processes:** Essential operations that directly deliver value to customers. Examples include **Design and Development, Manufacturing, and Sales**.
2. **Support Processes:** Back-office activities that provide value to internal customers and do not generate income themselves. Examples include **HR, IT Support, and Accounting**.
3. **Management Processes:** How a business plans, organizes, and controls resources. This includes **Strategic Planning and Budgeting**.

# 4. Functional vs. Cross-Functional

- **Functional:** Happens within a single department (e.g., a "Payroll" process entirely within HR).
- **Cross-Functional:** Involves multiple departments working toward a common goal. For example, **Order-to-Cash** involves Sales, Finance, and Delivery departments.
