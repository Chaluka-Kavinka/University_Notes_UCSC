**Process Analysis** is the methodology used to understand the health of various operations to improve efficiency and ensure processes align with organizational goals. It scrutinizes the "As-is" model to identify weaknesses and their impact on business outcomes.

# 1. Qualitative Analysis

This provides systematic insights into process issues to identify waste and prioritize improvements.

- **Value-Added Analysis:** Steps are classified as **Value-Adding (VA)**, **Business Value-Adding (BVA)** (necessary for regulations or smooth operations), or **Non-Value-Adding (NVA)** (waste like handovers, delays, and rework).
- **Root-Cause Analysis:** Analysts use **Cause-Effect (Fishbone) Diagrams** to group factors into the "6Ms" (Machine, Method, Material, Man, Measurement, Milieu) and **Why-Why Diagrams** to recursively probe for the source of a problem.
- **Issue Register & Pareto Analysis:** Identified problems are documented in a register; Pareto analysis identifies the "vital few" (20%) of issues responsible for the majority (80%) of problems.

# 2. Quantitative Analysis

This focuses on measurable data to provide a solid basis for decision-making regarding time, cost, and capacity.

- **Flow Analysis:** Used to calculate **Cycle Time** (the total time from start to end) and **Cycle Time Efficiency**, which measures the proportion of time spent on value-adding activities.
- **Queuing Theory:** Analyzes delays caused by resource contention, job size variation, and "bursty" traffic patterns where many cases arrive at once.
- **Process Simulation:** Generates a large number of hypothetical process instances to gather detailed statistics on duration, cost, and resource utilization.

# 3. The Devil’s Quadrangle

Analysts must balance four competing performance dimensions: **Time** (duration), **Cost** (expenses), **Quality** (accuracy), and **Flexibility** (adaptability). Improving one dimension often comes at the expense of another, creating a "devilish" optimization challenge.
